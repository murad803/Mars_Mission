Executive summary

The problem

Breathable air and rocket oxidizer are the biggest supply bottlenecks on Mars. NASA’s MOXIE uses gaseous solid oxide electrolysis, but Mars’s atmosphere is only about 6–7 millibars. Gas processing gives low output and needs lots of energy to pull in huge volumes of thin air.

The idea

LMOR compresses CO₂ to a liquid at 60 atmospheres, making it about 100 times denser. Suspended copper oxide (CuO) and gold nanoparticles, lit by laser through a sapphire window, split the carbon–oxygen bonds. Oxygen stays a gas and floats to the top for collection.

Intake & filtration

Two obstacles: very low pressure (6–7 millibars) and abrasive, electrostatic dust. Air must be cleaned and pre-compressed before it reaches the liquefaction chamber.

Dual-stage cyclonic dust separation

Dust averages 1.5 micrometers and carries a negative charge from solar UV. In the liquid reactor it would foul the nanocatalysts and block the laser light.

  • Primary cyclone: a fast conical swirl flings particles of 5+ micrometers into a waste bin.
  • Secondary electrostatic cyclone: a positively charged mesh pulls in the fine dust, reaching 99.9% filtration with no clogging filters.

Multi-stage cryogenic intake compressor

  1. Roots blower

    Compresses air from 0.006 to 0.5 atmospheres.

  2. Radiator cooling

    Martian cold (average −60 °C) removes heat, raising density and saving energy.

  3. Reciprocating pump

    Reaches 5 atmospheres and pushes gas through a one-way check valve into the liquefaction cylinder.

Materials & geometry

  • Wall: forged Titanium-Beta alloy (Ti-3Al-8V-6Cr-4Mo-4Zr), ductile well below 0 °C.
  • Coating: diamond-like carbon to reduce piston-ring wear.
  • Shape: vertical cylinder, exactly 10 liters at maximum compression.

Piston & seals

  • Piston head: same titanium alloy, flat face for even pressure.
  • Quad-ring seals: four self-lubricating Viton-GF seals with PTFE backup rings.
  • Actuator: dual-action hydraulic ram driven by a brushless DC motor, up to 150 kN.

The compression stroke

  1. Step 1

    Intake valve fills the 100 L chamber with CO₂ at 5 atm.

  2. Step 2

    Valve seals. The ram shrinks 100 L to 10 L (10:1).

  3. Step 3

    At 60 atm and a steady +20 °C, the gas condenses into clear liquid.

Nanocatalytic reaction

Inside the liquid CO₂, suspended nanoparticles act as an artificial photosynthesis system. The right wavelength of light breaks the bonds and releases pure oxygen.

Placeholder for laser window image

Sapphire window & laser matrix

  • Window: single-crystal aluminum oxide (Al₂O₃), clear to UV and visible light, with compressive strength over 2 GPa.
  • Seal: conical seat with a gold-foil gasket. Pressure pushes the sapphire tighter, so the joint seals itself.
  • Emitters: gallium nitride (GaN) laser diodes at 365 nm (UV-A) and 450 nm (deep blue), 750 W of continuous optical power.

The photocatalytic cycle

Each CuO nanocube (25 nm) carries gold clusters (1–2 nm) where CO₂ molecules attach.

  1. 1

    Adsorption. A liquid CO₂ molecule lands on a gold cluster.

  2. 2

    Photo-excitation. The 365 nm laser knocks an electron up in the CuO core, leaving a positive hole.

  3. 3

    Charge transfer. The electron jumps to the gold and into CO₂, destabilizing the C=O double bonds.

  4. 4

    Cleaving. The bond breaks. Oxygen atoms pair into O₂ gas. Carbon becomes solid carbon or carbon monoxide, depending on calibration.

Gas separation & mass-balance math

After splitting, oxygen and carbon must be separated and measured, all while the chamber stays at 60 atmospheres.

Buoyancy extraction

Liquid O₂ at +20 °C would need over 1,000 atm, so at 60 atm oxygen stays gas. Bubbles rise into a pocket under the piston face.

Micro-valve extraction

Infrared sensors track the liquid–gas boundary. When the pocket reaches 1.5 liters, a pneumatic needle valve releases O₂ into an expansion tank that steps it down to 1 atmosphere for breathing.

Carbon precipitation

Solid carbon sinks into the bottom cone. A Teflon scraping blade sweeps it every 10 minutes into a sealed airlock, ready for 3D-printing habitat parts or graphite components.

Safety, power & scale

A 60-atmosphere reactor on Mars needs redundant fail-safes and a modest, reliable power supply.

Blast shielding

The titanium cylinder is wrapped in a multi-layer Kevlar-29 sleeve that stops explosive fragmentation if it cracks.

Burst disc

A non-electric rupture disc set to 75 atmospheres vents excess CO₂ safely into the Martian air.

Auto-depressurization

Piezoelectric sensors watch for stress. If integrity drops, the piston retracts and pressure falls to baseline within 0.4 seconds.

Power budget: about 4.6 kW continuous

Small vertical solar arrays or a compact Kilopower fission reactor can supply this.

Scaling up

  • Modular: each unit is only about 1.2 m³. Three units in parallel add redundancy and secure over 100 kg of oxygen per hour.
  • Propellant: the oxygen can feed cryogenic chillers to make liquid oxygen (LOX) and refuel return rockets on the pad.
